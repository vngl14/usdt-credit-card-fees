## What you actually pay when you buy USDT with a credit card

Four separate charges can hit a single card purchase, and only one of them appears on the platform's checkout screen.

| Charge | Typical amount | Who collects it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card processing fee | 3–5% of the order | The platform or its payment processor |
| Cash advance fee | 2–5%, plus interest from day one | Your card issuer, if crypto counts as a cash advance |
| Bank transfer / ACH alternative | 0.5–1%, or $0 on ACH | Platform and bank |
| Network fee when you move the USDT later | Under $1 on TRC-20; roughly £2–20 on ERC-20 | The blockchain, paid in gas |

Banxa publishes the clearest version of this on its USDT page: a £500 card purchase lands as roughly £475–£485 of USDT, and if the issuer treats it as a cash advance the total cost climbs to 5–10%. At £10,000, the card route costs £300–£500 against £50–£100 by bank transfer. Paying £200–£400 for a few hours of speed is a decision you should make deliberately, not by default.

Card issuers are the wildcard. Whether crypto counts as a purchase or a cash advance depends on your card agreement, and the cash advance classification usually means no grace period, so interest accrues from the transaction date even if you pay the statement in full. One phone call to your issuer before a large order settles the question.

## The network choice that can cost you everything

USDT exists on several chains, and the one you pick at checkout has to match the wallet or exchange address you are sending to. Ethereum (ERC-20) has the widest DeFi support but gas of roughly £2–£20 per transfer afterwards. Tron (TRC-20) is cheaper and faster for plain transfers, usually under £1. Solana, BNB Smart Chain, and Polygon versions exist too.

Send ERC-20 USDT to a TRC-20 address and the funds are gone. There is no reversal, no support ticket that recovers them, no exception. Before you confirm, open the receiving wallet and check which network it displays for USDT, then match it exactly at checkout.

If the USDT is going straight into a DeFi position, ERC-20 avoids a bridge. If it is going to a friend or a payment processor, TRC-20 usually saves money on both ends. ## Is your USDT actually withdrawable right after you buy it?

Not always, and the gap between "it arrived" and "you can move it" catches people out. Binance.US, for example, shows card purchases instantly in your balance but applies a 7-day hold before the USD value can be withdrawn when you funded by ACH, debit card, or Apple Pay. MoonPay quotes card delivery in as little as 5 minutes and bank transfers in 1–3 business days, with the first-purchase review on top.

Two different clocks are running: when the crypto appears in your account, and when you can send it out. If you are buying USDT to pay someone today, confirm the withdrawal availability rule for your account and funding method before you pay the fee for speed. Paying 4% for instant delivery and then discovering a hold defeats the purpose.
